We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Strategic Planning and Institutional Resilience in Cairo's Health Sector: Examining the Mediating Role of Capacity Development in the Context of Environmental Crisis Management"**, for consideration in *Environment Systems and Decisions*. The manuscript comes to you through the Springer Nature transfer service.

Heatwaves, flooding and air pollution increasingly disrupt health services, and health institutions are expected to manage these environmental risks through planning. Yet there is little quantitative evidence on how such planning becomes resilience, particularly in North African megacities. Using a bilingual survey of 416 health-sector managers in Cairo, Egypt, we examined how environment-aware strategic planning, capacity development (training, earmarked resources, climate-relevant infrastructure and interdepartmental cooperation) and institutional resilience are related.

The main findings are:

- Strategic planning was associated with resilience almost entirely through capacity development (indirect β = 0.84, 95% CI 0.69–1.00), with no detectable direct association.
- The pattern held among screened respondents, after excluding uniform and possible repeat submissions, after adjusting for respondent characteristics, and after modelling a common-method factor.
- We report the study's limitations openly: capacity development and resilience are highly correlated as measured, a reverse-order model fits equally well, and responses cluster at the scale maximum.

The study is cross-sectional, so the manuscript describes the model explicitly as statistical mediation. It shows how the associations are structured; it does not establish causal order. The manuscript reports the equivalent reverse-order model and calls for longitudinal, multi-source studies to test causality.

We believe the work fits the journal's interest in risk, resilience and emergency readiness in environmental issues and in environmental management and decision-making. It treats environmental preparedness in health institutions as a management decision problem. It also shows that assessment and planning are linked to resilience mainly when they are translated into resourced capacity, which gives managers and regulators concrete, monitorable targets.
